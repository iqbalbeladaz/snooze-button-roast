## The entry

> My alarm clock rings at 6 AM on Saturday like a fire drill in a building nobody works in anymore — loud, pointless, and weirdly proud of itself.

One line, 144 characters (gate: 1–200).

## Why this line should win

- **It roasts the alarm, on the dot:** a 6 AM Saturday alarm, named in the first words — no other subject could be swapped in.
- **The punchline lands last:** "loud, pointless, and weirdly proud of itself" pays off in the final words, after the setup has done its work.
- **A fresh angle:** not "I hate mornings" and not "who invented this thing" — the alarm is a fire drill performing its emergency routine for an audience of nobody, in a building with no fire and no one to save.
- **Tight, one breath:** every word earns its place; the whole line is sayable out loud in a single breath.
- **Sharp and concrete:** a fire drill, an empty building, a beep that sounds proud — specific images, not vague grumbling.

## How it works

`main.py` writes the line to `/output/entry.txt`. It is fully
deterministic: the same bytes on every run, no input, no randomness, no
network.

```sh
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt
```
